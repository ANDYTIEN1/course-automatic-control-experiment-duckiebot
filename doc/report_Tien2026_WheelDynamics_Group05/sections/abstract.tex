\begin{abstract}
Linear first-order models are widely used to approximate direct-current motor dynamics for control design, yet practical actuators often exhibit nonlinearities such as dead zones, hysteresis, and operating-point-dependent dynamics. In this experiment, we investigate the open-loop rotational dynamics of the right drive motor (m10) of a Duckiebot DB17 (R5), driven through a 1600~Hz pulse-width-modulated motor driver. Wheel angle and speed are extracted from slow-motion video at 120 frames per second, synchronized with the motor commands by a light-emitting diode switched by the controller. We test three predictions of a linear frictionless first-order model with a duty-cycle sweep and with repeated step sequences $0 \to 0.6 \to 1.0 \to 0.6 \to 0$. At low duty cycles, the motor exhibits a dead zone rather than proportional rotation: on the positive sweep, the wheel first moves at a duty cycle of $\StartDuty$ but stops only at $\StopDuty$, indicating hysteresis. Furthermore, the fitted gain and time constant vary with the operating point: the gain drops by $\GainDrop\%$ from the step $0 \to 0.6$ to the step $0.6 \to 1.0$, and upward and downward steps show asymmetric transients rather than mirror symmetry. These results highlight the importance of accounting for dry friction and operating-point shifts when modeling low-cost wheeled robot actuators.
\end{abstract}
