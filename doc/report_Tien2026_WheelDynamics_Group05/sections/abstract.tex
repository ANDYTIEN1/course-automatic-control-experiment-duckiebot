\begin{abstract}
Linear first-order models are widely used to approximate DC motor dynamics for control design, yet practical actuators often exhibit nonlinearities such as dead zones, hysteresis, and operating-point-dependent dynamics. In this experiment, we investigate the open-loop rotational dynamics of both drive motors (m9 and m10) on Duckiebot DB17 (R5) powered by a $5.1\,\text{V}$ supply via a $1600\,\text{Hz}$ PWM motor driver. Wheel angular position and speed are extracted from $240\,\text{fps}$ slow-motion video synchronized with motor commands using a GPIO-driven LED. We test three predictions derived from a linear frictionless first-order model across a duty-cycle sweep (E1) and repeated step sequences $0 \to 0.6 \to 1.0 \to 0.6 \to 0$ (E2). At low duty cycles, both motors exhibit a dead zone rather than proportional rotation: on the increasing positive sweep, m9 and m10 first move at duties of $0.30$ and $0.26$, whereas stopping occurs at lower duty magnitudes ($0.22$ and $0.20$ on the positive sweep, and $-0.20$ and $-0.22$ after starting at $-0.28$ on the negative sweep), confirming rate-independent hysteresis. Furthermore, fitted first-order gain and time constant vary across operating points—gain drops by $30\%$ for m9 and $28\%$ for m10 from $0 \to 0.6$ to $0.6 \to 1.0$, and upward/downward step pairs show asymmetric transient behavior rather than mirror symmetry. These results highlight the importance of accounting for dry friction and operating-point shifts when modeling low-cost wheeled robot actuators.
\end{abstract}
