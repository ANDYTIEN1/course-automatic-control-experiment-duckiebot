\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

In control engineering, permanent-magnet direct-current (DC) motors driving robot wheels are commonly approximated as linear first-order time-invariant (LTI) systems \parencite{franklin2019FeedbackControlDynamic}. While this simplified representation facilitates controller design and transient analysis, physical actuators on mobile robots frequently depart from ideal linear behavior due to dry friction, actuator saturation, and operating-point-dependent effects \parencite{armstronghelouvry1994SurveyModelsAnalysis}.

In a differential-drive platform such as the Duckiebot \parencite{paull2017DuckietownOpenInexpensive}, unmodeled actuator nonlinearities or mismatches between the left and right wheel drives can degrade open-loop accuracy and transient consistency. Characterizing where a linear first-order model holds and where nonlinearities emerge is therefore essential before designing feedback controllers.

This experiment evaluates the open-loop rotational dynamics of the right drive motor (m10) of Duckiebot DB17 robot R5 using a duty-cycle sweep (E1) and repeated step sequences (E2) to test three predictions of an ideal linear model: proportional response without a dead zone (P1), operating-point-invariant gain and time constant (P2), and directional symmetry without hysteresis (P3). Section~\ref{sec:theory} derives the first-order motor model and testable predictions, Section~\ref{sec:method} details the experimental apparatus and video-based measurement procedure, Section~\ref{sec:results} presents the empirical findings, Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses them, and Section~\ref{sec:conclusions} concludes.
