\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

The measurements of R5's right-wheel motor m10 showed three limitations
of a single first-order linear model. First, a nonzero duty could leave
the wheel below the detectable-motion threshold, and the positive sweep
started at $u=\StartDuty$ but stopped at $u=\StopDuty$. Second, the
fitted gain decreased by $\GainDrop\%$ between the two upward step
intervals, while the time constant decreased by a factor of $\TauRatio$.
Third, the starting and stopping responses, and the upward and downward
steps between nonzero duties, were asymmetric. These observations
contradicted the predictions P1--P3 tested in the Results section.
A first-order fit described the dominant response of an individual
transition, but its parameters did not transfer across all tested duties
and motion histories. Tracking uncertainty limited the precision of the
low-duty thresholds, and the conclusions are restricted to this motor
under the tested stand conditions.
