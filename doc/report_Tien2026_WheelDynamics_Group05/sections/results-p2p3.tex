\subsection{P2: Gain and time constant at two operating points}
Across three repetitions, the right wheel reached $\UpSixFinal$ rad/s after the step from rest to $u=0.6$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:g5-step-06}). Extrapolating the fitted gain to full duty predicted $\PredOne$ rad/s. The measured full-duty speed was $\MeasOne$ rad/s (Fig.~\ref{fig:g5-step-10}). In the first-order fits without delay, the gain fell by $\GainDrop\%$ and the time constant fell from $\UpSixTau$ ms to $\UpTenTau$ ms (Table~\ref{tab:g5steps}). Both parameters varied with the operating point, contrary to P2.

For the first E2 trial, the uncertainty in aligning the video with the
motor commands using the LED was $\TimingSd$ ms. For the $0.6\to1.0$
duty step, the median per-frame uncertainty in wheel speed across the
three repetitions was $\OmegaSd$ rad/s.

\subsection{P3: Start--stop hysteresis and transient asymmetry}
On the positive sweep, the right wheel started at $u=\StartDuty$ and stopped at $u=\StopDuty$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:g5-hysteresis}a). On the negative sweep, the corresponding signed duties were $-\NegStartDuty$ and $-\NegStopDuty$. In both directions, the wheel kept rotating at a duty magnitude below the one required to start it.

Starting from rest gave a time constant of $\UpSixTau$ ms, whereas stopping from 0.6 duty gave $\DownZeroTau$ ms. The mirrored stop response therefore rose faster in the speed-change plot (Fig.~\ref{fig:g5-hysteresis}b). Between the two nonzero duty levels, the ordering reversed: the downward step had a time constant of $\DownSixTau$ ms, compared with $\UpTenTau$ ms for the upward step. These paired responses departed from the mirror symmetry predicted by P3.
