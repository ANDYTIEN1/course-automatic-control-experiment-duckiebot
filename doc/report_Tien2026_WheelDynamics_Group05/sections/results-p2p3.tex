\subsection{P2: Gain and time constant at two operating points}
Across three E2 repetitions per motor, the fitted gain decreased when the step changed from $0\to0.6$ to $0.6\to1.0$ (Table~\ref{tab:g5steps}). The decrease was 30\% for m9 and 28\% for m10. The higher-duty step also had a shorter time constant on both wheels. At full duty the measured steady speeds were 25.5 and 25.0 rad/s, below the respective predictions of 28.7 and 28.0 rad/s obtained by extrapolating the lower-duty fits. A single gain and time constant did not describe both operating points (Figs.~\ref{fig:g5-step-06} and~\ref{fig:g5-step-10}).

\subsection{P3: Direction dependence and hysteresis}
On the positive sweep, m9 started at $u=0.30$ and stopped at 0.22, while m10 started at 0.26 and stopped at 0.20. On the negative sweep, the corresponding signed start and stop duties were $-0.28$ and $-0.20$ for m9 and $-0.28$ and $-0.22$ for m10. Thus, each motor continued rotating at duty magnitudes below those required to start it. The m9 negative sweep carried a tracking warning: the steady-angle scatter was 10.4 times its pixel-based uncertainty. Its thresholds warrant greater caution.

The transient asymmetry depended on the pair of steps (Fig.~\ref{fig:g5-hysteresis}). For m9, stopping from 0.6 gave $\tau=59.8$ ms, compared with 209.2 ms for starting. For m10 the corresponding values were 56.2 and 203.0 ms. Between the two nonzero levels the ordering reversed: stepping down from 1.0 to 0.6 took longer than stepping up from 0.6 to 1.0 (256.5 versus 82.5 ms for m9, and 230.3 versus 74.2 ms for m10). Neither pair followed the mirror symmetry predicted by P3.
